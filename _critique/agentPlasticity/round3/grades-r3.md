## Rules used (the same as round 1)

- 2: the narration states the answer and the viewer reports it. A hedge tag on content the narration states does not lower the score.
- 1: the narration states part of the answer. The viewer supplies the rest from a [guess], from [my background], or from a label or picture that the viewer says they would miss at speaking pace.
- 0: the narration states none of the answer, or the viewer holds a misreading from the key.
- Clauses that the question does not ask (what the dollars pay for, how many models, the same practice) are recorded as not conveyed and are not scored.
- Facts marked "bonus" (novice) or "(small)" or "(picture)" (engineer) do not count. Neither viewer used them to raise a mark.
- Q1, Q11 and Q12 are judged as the key describes them. Q12 passes at four of the six items.

Why each wording:

- l04. "In chess" says that the example is one game's run, which fits "or" in l03 and the label "Each game has its own rounds". The example for Q4 stays in the narration ("a program", in chess).
- l06. The exam clause is the reason the viewers could not hear. The novice supplied "a test counts when you did not practice on it" from school, and this puts that idea in the narration. It has not been tested on anyone. "Evidence of progress" keeps the paper's own word (p. 6).
- l08. The pair of l01 comes first (Sol and Luna). The third model arrives as an addition ("too") and is not shown by an elliptical verb. "With the same practice" leaves l08. It stays in l01, and it is an unasked clause for Q7.
- l11. "Bad moves" is a plain name that needs no definition. The 12 words are the same.
- l12. "Which" matches the paper: it names candidates and has not separated them. Using "if" read as "does not know whether they are weak at all". "Bad moves" ties "weak" to the l11 mistakes. "Poor use" means how well, so it no longer clashes with "used" in l10. "Why?" is gone, so nothing is left without an object.
- l13. The subject is the numbers, not the measure. "Plasticity does not rank models" would follow "Sol had the best plasticity" and sound like a contradiction, and the paper says plasticity is not a universal ranking, not that it ranks nothing. "Plasticity numbers" gives "these" a spoken antecedent. "Estimates" stays on the l09 label.

Cuts forced by the 195-word cap (not viewer findings): l01 "but" (two short sentences), l02 "AI", l03 "AI", l05 "new", l09 "ended with" to "reached". Each is one word. The l09 change overrides my answer to question 3 in `paper-faithful-r3.md` ("do not change the narration"): the cap forces it, and "reached" keeps the endpoint sense.

No narration change for l10. The 98% misreading (all of Luna's moves) is probably small in size: the paper says relevant moves are about 98% of chess moves after round 0, pooled over all models, and it does not report Luna's own share. I still do not rely on that. The fix is in the labels:
