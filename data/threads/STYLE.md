# Threads thread style

Learned from three threads: @hemanthvasi Dream-RSI (4 posts) and WikiSkill (5 posts), and a 3-post summary thread by @omarsar0. Post 1 carries the image. Every later post is a reply to the one before it.

## Shape (4 to 6 posts)
1. Hook, image, arXiv link. One claim or tension from the paper in plain words, then the paper name and who wrote it. The link goes in the text (image posts cannot hold a link card).
2. The problem. What fails today, with the paper's own evidence and its scope (which run, which task).
3. The idea. How the method works, in two or three short sentences.
4. Results. Two or three numbers, each with its setup: which task, which model, how many runs, what it is compared with.
5. Limits and a question. What the paper did not test or show. End with one question to the reader. Add the Instagram reel link as the last paragraph, after the question.

## Rules
- Plain, specific, slightly terse. No hype words (game-changer, massive, revolutionary, "the craziest finding"). No em-dashes or en-dashes. No emoji unless the paper's field uses them. No 🧵.
- Each post is at most 500 bytes (UTF-8). Aim for 250 to 400.
- Every number comes from the paper text. Say what it is: a mean of three runs, one run, a baseline run and not the paper's method, a token share and not a cost share.
- Keep the paper's hedges. Do not turn "may" into "does", an average into a general result, or a best case into a typical one.
- Do not claim a cause the paper did not test. Say what the ablations change and what they do not.
- Name the lab or university only as the title page gives it.
- One topic_tag, 1 to 50 characters, no periods or ampersands. "AI Agents" is the default.
- The last post ends with `Reel: {{IG_URL}}` on its own line. The skill replaces the placeholder with the Instagram reel link. If no reel is live, the skill drops that line.
