# What the paper says

- A CLM treats the context as a file the model may edit without restriction, using Bash. Edits sync to the live context; with no edit, new tokens are appended.[^pdf]
- Zero-shot on BrowseComp-Plus with Qwen3.6-27B and a 32K context limit, CLM scores 59.4%, 11.4% (relative) above the strongest baseline, Codex-style summarization, with 21.5% fewer prefix-reuse FLOPs.[^pdf]
- On TBLite, 73.7% vs 67.0% for summarization, at 91% of its FLOPs. On TerminalBench 2.1 it matches summarization at 70% of its FLOPs.[^pdf]
- EdgeBench-10 (12 hours, Qwen3.6-27B): CLM 44.6 at 179 prefix-reuse PFLOPs vs 42.3 at 437 for summarization. The abstract states 5% higher score with 59% fewer FLOPs.[^pdf]
- Software World, a 24-hour six-repository agent swarm (GPT-5.6-Sol, 272K context): 65% greater downstream speedup than a summary-based swarm at the same spend.[^pdf]
- Reinforcement learning on Qwen3.5-9B: BrowseComp-Plus 28.8% to 42.5% (+13.7 points), 1.34 vs 2.19 PFLOPs per question for the trained summary harness.[^pdf]
- Suffix Cache Reuse serves BrowseComp-Plus at 65.0% of standard SGLang's empirical prefix-reuse FLOPs (abstract: 35% less server-side compute).[^pdf]
- Skill evolution on ContextBench improves held-out accuracy by up to 35.9 points.[^pdf]
- Safety: editable context can carry prompt injections across turns; defenses are left to future work.[^pdf]
- "FLOPs" are the authors' prefix-reuse FLOPs, a theoretical measure, not measured time or dollars.[^pdf]
