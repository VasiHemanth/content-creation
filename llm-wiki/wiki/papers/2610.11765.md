# Thinking Inertia

Topic: [LLM reasoning control](/wiki/topics/llm-reasoning-control.md). Authors: Dianqiao Lei, Kevin Qinghong Lin, Pan Lu, Philip Torr, James Zou (Tsinghua, Oxford, Stanford). arXiv 2610.11765v1, 8 Oct 2026.

## 1. Identity
"Thinking Inertia: LLMs Keep Thinking When Told Not To", arXiv 2610.11765, version v1 (the only version on 2026-10-15, and the one the video was made from). Preprint, not peer reviewed.[^paper]

## 2. What problem does it address?
Whether a model with its thinking mode off, or told to answer without reasoning, actually stops writing reasoning before its answer, and how to measure that from the visible response instead of from proxies such as a switch setting, a missing `<think>` block or a short output.[^paper]

## 3. What is the method?
Each response is split into visible pre-answer text and the final answer, then scored at three levels: Empty-Thinking Rate (strict answer-only compliance), Question-Pre-answer Relevance (embedding similarity) and Explicit Inference Rate (a blinded GPT-5.5 judge labels the pre-answer text; only deduction, computation, comparison, elimination and similar steps count). Six models (Qwen3-4B, 32B, 235B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-5.4, Gemini-3-Flash), six benchmarks in three answer spaces (yes-or-no: BoolQ, StrategyQA; multiple choice: MMLU, MMLU-Pro; open-ended math: GSM8K, MATH), and six modes from thinking-on to a prefilled answer. The two modes that matter most: M2, thinking mode off with no prompt change, and M5, thinking mode off plus "Give the final answer directly without any reasoning." Gemini-3-Flash runs at "minimal" rather than off in M2 to M6.[^paper]

## 4. What is the evidence?
All results are the authors' runs, temperature 0.[^paper]
- Thinking mode off (M2), explicit inference pooled over all outputs (Table D.3): yes-or-no 5.15%, multiple choice 45.57%, open-ended math 99.69%. Per model on open-ended it is 99.5 to 99.9% (H.1, H.2), so the open-ended figure holds for every model. Multiple choice varies widely per model (GPT-5.4 5.3%, Gemini 88.9%); the six-model mean is about 55%, higher than the pooled 45.57%, because the local Qwen runs with full splits weigh more in the pool.
- Strict prompt (M5), same tasks and judge: open-ended 62.00% pooled. Per model it varies: Qwen3-4B 97.78%, Qwen3-235B 98.90%, Qwen3-32B 56.73% (H.2); GPT-5.4 is almost fully answer-only (M5 ETR 100 and 99.8 on GSM8K and MATH, G.2).
- Accuracy: across the five models with fully disabled thinking, M2 to M5 lowers mean open-ended accuracy by about 15 percentage points and raises mean answer-only compliance from near zero to about 40%. Per model the drop ranges from about zero (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-235B) to 49 points (Qwen3-32B on GSM8K). Yes-or-no and multiple choice show little accuracy change.
- Same question, three answer spaces (Fig 6, Qwen3-32B, thinking mode off, matched numeric MMLU items): rewriting multiple choice as yes-or-no lowers explicit inference from 86.95% to 36.90% at about the same accuracy; open-ended raises it to 90.14% and lowers accuracy to 52.6%. The p. 13 figure shows one item where, under the strict prompt, the yes-or-no and multiple-choice forms get only the answer and the open-ended form gets the full solution.
- Judge checks: Claude Opus 4.8 on the full corpus gives 98.55% (M2) and 61.02% (M5) on open-ended; five human annotators agree with GPT-5.5 on 98.8% of 600 outputs (binary).

## 5. What does it not show?
- Anything about computation inside the model. "Our claims therefore concern visible output, not the absence of task-relevant computation from hidden states" (sec 5.1). Answer-only output is not shown to save compute (sec 5.2).
- Settings beyond the suite: "open-ended" here means two math benchmarks. Code, tool use, multi-turn, multilingual and sampled decoding are untested.
- A per-model guarantee for the strict prompt: the pooled 62% hides a spread from almost full compliance (GPT-5.4) to almost none (Qwen3-4B).
- Gemini-3-Flash never had thinking fully off; it is outside the 15-point average.
- EIR depends on the rubric and the judge, though a second judge and human annotators track it closely.

## 6. One experiment that would test the main claim
Run the same M2 and M5 comparison on code generation and multi-turn tool use with sampled decoding, and record reasoning-token counts where the provider reports them. If explicit inference stays high on open-ended code while answer-only output does not lower reported reasoning tokens, the claim that visible compliance says little about cost gets direct support.

## 7. What should be read next?
Table D.3 and Figure G.2 (pooled rates against per-model accuracy changes), Figure 6 and Table I.5 (answer-space rewrites), and the p. 13 example. On this wiki: [Meta-Reasoning (Thinking Before Thinking)](/wiki/papers/2609.38147.md), which is about how an agent spends its calls, not whether a model stops writing steps.

## 8. Sharpest teaching angle for a 60 second video
Ask the title as a question: if you tell AI models not to think, do they stop? Keep the two conditions apart: thinking mode off with no extra prompt (99.7% of open-ended math replies still show explicit inference, true for each model) and a strict "no reasoning" prompt (62% pooled, varies by model, and about 15 points lower mean accuracy across five models). Then the answer-space idea with the one p. 13 example: when the answer is on the page, the model points; when it is not, it works it out. Close on the limit: visible output only.
