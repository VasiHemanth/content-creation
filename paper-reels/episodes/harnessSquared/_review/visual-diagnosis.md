# Harness Squared: visual diagnosis

Read-only review. Sources: storyboard.json, all 9 scene files, out/hf/validation-sheet.png, 1 fps frames from final.mp4 (81 s), the same sheets for statelessLanguageAgents and thinkingInertia, hf/README.md, formats/picture.md, formats/paper.md. Times are approximate, read off 1 fps frames.

## What is on screen, in numbers

| | harnessSquared | statelessLanguageAgents | thinkingInertia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene HTML size (bytes, 9/10/7 scenes) | 4.2k to 7k | 6.7k to 13k | 6.2k to 15k |
| Timeline calls per scene | 12 to 32 | 18 to 48 | 14 to 51 |
| Distinct drawn metaphors | 3 sprites total | about 9 | about 7 |
| Scenes using the same robot | 4 of 9 (hook, probe x2, reset, close) | 5 of 10, but each one does a different job | 1 of 7 |
| Scenes with the same "page with grey lines" or "folder" | 5 of 9 (task cards, fail memo, payoff memo, compose folder, reset cabinet) | none repeated | none repeated |
| Receipts | 2, both full black frame with a thin strip | 3, over a dimmed live scene | 4, over a dimmed live scene |

The 3 sprites: a small robot (about 150 px wide) standing on a line, a white A4 page with grey lines, a navy folder with a tab. Every scene is one of these plus 2 to 5 small tag labels.

Text: tags are 26 to 36 px mono. Headline stamps (43, 78, +11.5, PROBE) are 72 to 140 px. The frame's top 160 px and bottom third of the content band are empty in most scenes. In hook, task, probe, compose, reset and close the object occupies roughly 25 to 30 percent of the 1080 x 1290 content area.

Motion: every scene gets the same camera move (scale 1.0 to 1.05 or 1.07, linear or sine, whole scene) plus `KIT.beats` pulses of 0.02. Element motion is entrances only (`pop`, `rise`, `slam`) and a few `shake`s. Nothing travels from A to B except the lesson card in reset (one 340 px rise). No packet, no hand-off, no counting, no layout change.

## (b) The 5 most damaging problems, ranked

1. The same three sprites carry the whole video. Robot, page, folder. The viewer sees "a robot and a document" for 80 s, so no idea feels new. Every scene should have its own drawn object.
2. Nothing in the pictures shows the mechanism. Two loops, probe, compare/combine, reset-and-save-lesson are tags next to objects, not events the viewer watches happen. Voice and picture carry two messages.
3. The two receipts are a blank frame with a 50 px strip, shown for about 5 s each as the only picture, with the scene hidden (world opacity 0). The crop is clipped on the left and right. The guide says one phrase, about 2 s, then cut back.
4. Text is the content. Tags at 26 to 36 px carry "work habits (invariant)", "job rules (domain)", "cumulative clutter & regressions", "evidence-backed edits". Combined with captions, a phone viewer reads small mono text and sees a small drawing.
5. Dead and static stretches. Empty frames at the start of task (about 1.5 s), compose (about 1.5 s), and one identical slow camera push for all 9 scenes. The validator passes because it counts any change, including a caption word turning amber.

## (c) How the reference episodes do it

- statelessLanguageAgents `hook`: a building under construction beside a token dial (the billion tokens is a tower you can see grow).
- `stall`: a step chart drawn from the data, with a stopwatch and a marker at the last improvement, then three robots on a rail for "no tool calls".
- `office`/`why`: the abstract "conversation" is a physical stack on a tray that falls away (`tl.to(tray... y:150, rotation:14` on "loses"), against a persistent research-state board. The idea ("loses the conversation, not the work") is a thing that visibly drops while another stays.
- `assign`: an Advisor in a hard hat hands a different icon to each of three Workers; then the "same feature" is three Workers building the same brick wall. The idea is seen, not labelled.
- `cost`: a stopwatch plus a magnifier on a sliver of a bar for "<0.6%".
- thinkingInertia `inertia`: carts roll along three rails to marked distances, slips bounce onto the third rail (`bounce.out`) for 99.7%, with a camera dive (`scale s5`, 1.2 s) from rail to a number. `strict`: a seesaw tilts for the accuracy drop. `forms`: three answer sheets in the same slot, with a pen sketch of the long answer.
- Receipts: both references keep a dimmed live scene behind the receipt (`#of-dim` 0.4, `#inertia-dim` 0.88) and cut back to a stamp or a new beat (`#inertia-stamp`). The strip still appears, but the scene stays present and the hold is short.
- Both references have 10 to 17 timeline paths per scene using real motion (rotation, bounce, travel, wipe, count), roughly twice the code per scene.

## (d) What a deterministic check could catch

1. Sprite reuse across scenes: hash each scene's SVG or DOM structure (or the shapes by class) and fail if more than 2 scenes share the same drawing ID, e.g. more than 2 calls to `KIT.robot` or the same `.memo` markup in different scenes. Here: `KIT.robot` in 4 scenes.
2. Empty frame area: for sampled frames inside the content band (y 160 to 1450), compute the fraction of pixels within 8 of the background colour and the largest empty bounding box. Fail if more than 70 percent of the band is background for more than 1 s, or if the content bounding box is less than 35 percent of the band for a whole scene. Would hit task, compose, probe, close, and all receipts.
3. Blank-frame starts: fail a scene whose first 0.8 s has no non-background, non-caption pixels (the task and compose openings).
4. Receipt frames: for receipt windows, fail if the content bbox height is under 15 percent of the band, if the frame mean luminance is under a threshold (black frame), if the hold is over 2.5 s, or if the crop edge touches the frame edge with text cut (check the pdf crop text vs the visible window: first and last visible words must be whole words). Flag a crop whose first visible character is not preceded by a space.
5. Minimum text size: from the DOM, fail any non-caption element under 30 px, or require that at least 50 percent of the non-caption text area is at 40 px or more. Fail a tag that overflows the 1080 width (the +11.5 tag, "Table 1" clip).
6. Camera variety: fail if more than half the scenes use the same scale tween (`1 to 1.05..1.07`) and no scene has a pan, a dive, or a layout change.
7. Event type diversity: count distinct kinds of motion per scene (travel, rotate, count, wipe, draw, layout change vs entrance only). Fail a scene with only `pop`/`rise` entrances and a camera drift.
8. Unlabelled objects: fail decorative blocks with no `data-bind` text or no relation to any spoken word (the 4 hook chips).
