# What the paper says (v3)

- Question (sec 1, p. 2): "When can developers use System One model judgments and probabilities to automate security checks in agentic software?" A System One model takes an input and a developer-defined question and assigns probabilities to predefined answers such as safe or unsafe. A security policy turns the unsafe probability into allow, block, or escalate for review, for example to "a second judge, another model that reassesses the input".[^p1][^p2]
- Models (sec 3.1, Table 1, p. 5): four System One models (Jev 1.13 hosted, Laya, Decider about 2B, Bespoke Nimble about 9B), two prompted judges (GPT-4.1, Qwen3-8B with thinking off), four specialists (ProtectAI DeBERTa v2, PIGuard, Prompt Guard 2 86M, WildGuard), the base models of Decider and Nimble, and five reasoning judges (GLM-5.3-Flash, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3.8-27B).[^p4][^p5]
- Tasks and data (sec 3.2, Table 2, p. 4 to 5): WAInjectBench-text (prompt injection), R-Judge (interaction risk), AgentHarm (harmful requests). WAInjectBench marks attack-derived text as containing explicit instructions (EI) or not (no-EI); both keep their unsafe labels. Partitions: development (recalibration fit), selection (threshold choice), confirmation and test (checked without retuning). Test sizes: 1,612 WAInjectBench, 236 R-Judge, 352 AgentHarm. AgentHarm has no selection or confirmation split and is left out of the policy experiment.[^p4][^p5]
- Classification (sec 4.2, Tables 3 to 5, p. 7 to 8): no primary configuration has the best Macro-F1 on all three tasks. On WAInjectBench, Nimble ranks first yet misses 8.1% of EI attacks and 92.3% of no-EI attacks; "Jev misses all 130 unsafe no-EI inputs while falsely flagging just one benign input." Finding 1: a model can rank highly overall while missing most attacks in a particular group. None of the 16 WAInjectBench FNR/FPR differences is significant after Holm adjustment (twelve source clusters). On AgentHarm Jev misses 14 of 176 harmful requests and flags 42 of 176 benign; GPT-4.1 misses one and flags 85. Finding 2: adapted models beat their base models on some tasks and lose on others.[^p7][^p8]
- Reasoning judges (sec 4.3, p. 9): all five beat Jev's R-Judge Macro-F1 point estimate, DeepSeek highest. On AgentHarm, Kimi misses none of 176 harmful requests but flags 96 benign. Finding 3: fewer missed harmful requests can come with more false alarms.[^p9]
- Calibration (sec 5.2, p. 12): "Jev assigns unsafe probabilities below 0.1 to 1,446 WAInjectBench inputs. Their mean predicted probability is 0.205%, yet 133 (9.20%) are unsafe. Of these 133 unsafe inputs, 130 lack direct attack instructions (no-EI). Removing the no-EI groups leaves an unsafe fraction of 0.228% and a mean unsafe probability of 0.176%." Finding 4: inputs assigned very low unsafe probabilities can still include many missed attacks. At max class probability of 0.95 or more, Jev has 130 WAInjectBench false negatives (p. 14).[^p12][^p14]
- Recalibration (sec 5.4, p. 15 to 16): temperature scaling improves test NLL in six of twelve System One combinations; on AgentHarm a temperature near 0.05 raises Jev's test NLL from 0.481 to 2.163. Tied original scores stay tied after scaling, so a threshold must treat them together.[^p15][^p16]
- Policy (sec 6.1, eq. 1, p. 17): allow if p < tau_L, block if p > tau_H, escalate if p is in [tau_L, tau_H]. "Inputs exactly at either threshold, or without usable scores, also escalate for review." Automatic coverage is the share allowed or blocked. Fig. 3 (p. 6) shows a rule with thresholds 10.5% and 89.5%.[^p6][^p17]
- Threshold search (sec 6.2, p. 17): 101 symmetric pairs; for each miss limit epsilon in {0.01, 0.05, 0.10} the pair with the highest selection coverage subject to Miss_auto <= epsilon and FB_auto <= 0.05. The search includes (0, 1), which escalates everything. A 1% miss limit permits one miss among WAInjectBench's 167 unsafe selection inputs and none among R-Judge's 74.[^p17]
- Symmetric policies at the 1% selection miss limit (sec 6.3, Table 13, p. 17 to 18): Jev, Decider, GPT-4.1 and Qwen3-8B send every WAInjectBench input for review (coverage 0.00%). Laya automates 5.02% but exceeds the miss limit on test; on R-Judge, Nimble passes confirmation but exceeds the block limit on test. Jev's 7.63% R-Judge coverage is 17 blocks and one allow.[^p17][^p18]
- Independent thresholds (sec 6.4, Table 14, p. 18 to 20): on R-Judge Jev's coverage rises from 7.63% to 47.03% (one allow, 110 blocks); "On WAInjectBench, coverage rises from zero to 6.70%, entirely through 108 blocks" (tau_L = 0.000, tau_H = 0.525, passes confirmation and test). Finding 5: independent thresholds can raise the number of blocked inputs while leaving the number allowed unchanged. Seven independent policies pass both checks, against nine symmetric ones.[^p19][^p20]
- Ties (sec 6.5, p. 21): "Thresholds cannot separate correct and incorrect decisions with the same score. Jev assigns p_i = 0 to 1,320 shared WAInjectBench inputs, including 102 misses ... Any positive allow threshold admits all zero-score inputs, including the missed attacks."[^p21]
- Second judges (sec 6.6, p. 21 to 23): "A judge can correct the first model's missed attack only when the review rule forwards that input." Jev misses 167 WAInjectBench unsafe inputs; seven judges correct between 8 and 32. "Among misses with max(p_i, 1 - p_i) >= 0.95, each judge detects at most five of Jev's 130 misses and 30 of Decider's 162," and a rule accepting predictions at that confidence "would send none of these misses for review". "Reviewing only uncertain inputs leaves confident mistakes unreviewed, even when another judge could detect them." On AgentHarm, Kimi corrects all 14 Jev misses but adds 54 false positives.[^p21][^p22][^p23]
- Implications (sec 7.2, p. 23 to 24): define the security task first; validate labels, probabilities and policy actions separately; check which missed unsafe inputs the review rule forwards; "errors in the former [automatically handled inputs] may never reach the second judge"; enforce permissions independently of model judgments.[^p23][^p24]
- Threats (sec 8, p. 24): the EI/no-EI, threshold-sensitivity and cross-model error analyses "are exploratory"; the grid restricts tau_L <= 0.5 and tau_H >= 0.5; rare errors and few source clusters limit interval precision; public benchmarks may overlap training data; "Results may differ for adaptive attacks, other languages, and multimodal inputs."[^p24]

# Changes from v1 that matter for the video

- Abstract finding (1) is reworded. v1: "Strong overall performance and favorable average calibration can hide systematic failures on particular attack groups, including attacks that models confidently classify as safe." v3: "High overall accuracy and low average calibration error can conceal attacks classified as safe with high confidence within particular groups."
- v1 sec 6.6 (v1 p. 23) ended "Confidently allowed attacks never reach the second judge, including those it could detect." v3 drops that sentence. It says instead that a judge can correct a miss "only when the review rule forwards that input" (p. 21) and "Reviewing only uncertain inputs leaves confident mistakes unreviewed, even when another judge could detect them" (p. 22).
- Counts shift by one or two inputs: the low-score bin is 1,446 inputs with 133 unsafe (9.20%) in v3, against 1,444 and 9.14% in v1; no-EI misses 130 (v1 129); zero-score group 1,320 with 102 misses (v1 1,318 and 101); Jev's WAInjectBench misses 167 (v1 166). The 0.205%, 0.228%, 0.176%, 6.70% (108 blocks) and 0.00% symmetric coverage are unchanged.
- v3 states directly that 130 of the 133 low-score unsafe inputs are no-EI. v1 said only that the gap was "largely due to" no-EI attacks.
- v1 called the EI/no-EI, independent-threshold and error-complementarity analyses "added after the primary evaluation"; v3 calls them "exploratory".
- v1's external-validity sentence ("covers known attack families and text inputs, leaving transfer to unseen or adaptive attacks ... open") becomes "Results may differ for adaptive attacks, other languages, and multimodal inputs."
- R-Judge results in v3 use all 236 test records (v1 ranked on 108 shared records), so R-Judge ranks and calibration figures differ.
- Tables are renumbered (v1 Table 19 and 20 are v3 Table 13 and 14; v1 Table 22 is v3 Table 16).
