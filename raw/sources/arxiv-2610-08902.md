# What the paper says

- Problem: most agent evaluations measure what an agent can do at a fixed point in time, not how effectively it learns from experience.[^p1]
- Protocol (sec 2.1): model weights stay frozen. Each acting episode begins in a fresh context; future instances inherit a persistent artifact inventory (executable Python tools, strategies or skills, textual memory) refined from training interactions. Proposed updates must pass structural and executable checks; rejected attempts still count toward learning cost.[^p3]
- Evaluation splits (sec 2.1): training interactions (visible to reflection), held-out in-distribution (ID) interactions (same difficulty regime), and held-out out-of-distribution (OOD) interactions (stronger opponents). Held-out trajectories and scores are never shown to reflection.[^p4]
- Learning cost (sec 2.1): dollars from token usage, covering both the acting phase and the reflection phase, including rejected artifact revisions. The paper calls it a proxy for compute, not physical computation.[^p4]
- Agent plasticity (abstract, sec 2.2): "the efficiency with which an agent converts experience into gains in future held-out performance." Average plasticity is held-out score gain divided by cumulative learning cost, reported in score points per $1,000. The headline measure, Psat, divides the fitted rise to 90% of saturation by the cost of reaching it (eq. 5); a fitted rise under 10 points is assigned Psat = 0.[^p1][^p5]
- Setup (sec 3.1): Chess (Hard and Easy), 5x5 Go, 6x6 Hex, and NetHack. Eight frontier models as fixed-weight lineages, 21 checkpoints (0-20) for board games, 41 (0-40) for NetHack. For NetHack the native Claude Code and Codex harnesses are the starting point, so the paper says it measures the plasticity of the resulting agents.[^p6]
- Finding 1 (sec 3.2-3.3): persistent self-improvement is model- and task-dependent. In Hard chess, Claude Fable 5 and Claude Opus 5 rise from 37.5% and 25.0% at checkpoint 0 to 73.3% and 66.4% averaged over checkpoints 16-20; GPT-5.6 Sol starts near 0% and gains about as much (to 36.9%), trailing in absolute score rather than improvement. Several models stay flat or decline. "The common learning interface therefore permits improvement but does not ensure it."[^p6]
- Finding 2 (sec 3.4): endpoint capability and acquisition efficiency diverge. On the combined chess+Go+Hex curve, GPT-5.6 Sol has the highest estimated Psat at 298 percentage points per $1,000 of mean per-game learning cost, followed by Claude Opus 5 at 233, Claude Fable 5 at 57, and Claude Opus 4.8 at 14; GPT-5.6 Luna is assigned Psat = 0 and GPT-5.5 does not saturate in its runs. Claude Fable 5 reaches the highest fitted endpoint.[^p7]
- Finding 3 (sec 3.5): artifact reuse is associated with improvement but is not a proxy for capability. Pooled over both chess conditions, GPT-5.6 Luna takes about 98% of relevant decisions without artifact use, Gemini 3.1 Pro about 87%, Claude Opus 4.6 about 51%, GPT-5.5 about 28%, against 2-6% for Claude Fable 5, Claude Opus 5, Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.6 Sol. High-reuse models still fail: 83-99% of their remaining failures happen while a relevant artifact is in use.[^p8]
- Concrete examples (sec 3.5): one Claude Fable 5 lineage writes its own chess engine and keeps improving it after specific mistakes; in one GPT-5.5 run, the reflection step keeps telling the agent to load its saved move-picking program right away, yet the agent still spends its first steps listing and reading files: "Writing down an instruction is not the same as following it."[^p9]
- NetHack (sec 3.3): Claude Opus 5.5's mean score grows from about 2k (checkpoints 0-2) to about 6k (checkpoints 36-40), Psat = 61.75 normalized points per $1,000; GPT-5.6 Luna and GPT-5.6 Sol decline.[^p7]
- Discussion (sec 4): plasticity is a property of an agent under a particular learning protocol, not an intrinsic scalar property of the underlying model; "we interpret plasticity as a protocol-dependent measure, not a universal model ranking" (sec 3.4). Estimates depend on score headroom, the learning horizon, saturation-fit assumptions, and provider pricing. The failure analysis is observational, not causal. The setup does not separate the value of environment feedback from the extra compute used to analyze it.[^p10]
