# Meta-Reasoning (Thinking Before Thinking)

Topic: [agent context management](/wiki/topics/agent-context-management.md). Authors: Paras Dahal, Anton Bakhtin, Taco Cohen and 9 others (Meta Superintelligence Labs). arXiv 2609.38147v1, stamped 29 Sep 2026 (the PDF prints 30 Sep 2026).

## 1. Identity
"Thinking Before Thinking: Scaling Agentic Inference Through Meta-Reasoning", arXiv 2609.38147, version v1 (the version the video was made from).[^paper]

## 2. What problem does it address?
On a long agent run, deciding what to do next (build on which partial result, start fresh, check, or stop) is a job of its own, and most agents make that decision in one step over a growing history while also doing the work.[^paper]

## 3. What is the method?
An inference-time harness, no training. Workers do the task: a single model call on the reasoning benchmarks, a coding agent with tools on ProgramBench. A controller only decides what work comes next. Each control cycle runs four stages, each its own agentic process with prompts and tools: Assess (rewrite a compact textual state of what the run has established), Propose (list candidate next computations, without seeing the budget), Evaluate (pick one against the remaining budget, or stop) and Dispatch (write the worker instruction and choose which stored artifacts it receives). Every output is stored in persistent memory as an artifact with an identifier; only the compact state carries over between cycles. Controller calls are charged against the same call budget as worker calls. The run is recorded as an artifact graph (an edge when one artifact is context for another), which the authors use for diagnostics: budget use, graph size, coverage of correct candidates, monitoring and selection.[^paper]

The matched baseline is a Direct Control Agent with the same workers, interfaces and budget that chooses each action in one call over its full accumulated history. The comparison changes staging, state compression and memory access together.[^paper]

## 4. What is the evidence?
All results are the authors' runs, with Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8, on IMO ProofBench-Advanced (30 problems), ARC-AGI-2 (120), LongCoT-mini (507) and ProgramBench (200).[^paper]
- Main budgets (100 calls reasoning, 1200 ProgramBench): higher point estimates in all 12 matched comparisons. Per-benchmark mean gains over the three models: IMO 4.0, ARC-AGI-2 4.2, LongCoT-mini 3.6 points. Per-model gains run from 0.4 (GPT-5.5, LongCoT-mini) to 9.2 (Gemini, LongCoT-mini).
- ProgramBench, mean per-problem test-pass rate: GPT-5.5 71.5% against 63.7% direct control (Codex 58.0%); Opus 4.8 67.2% against 65.3% (Claude Code 65.5%); Gemini 3.1 Pro 48.7% against 46.9%.
- Budget sweeps: with GPT-5.5 on ProgramBench, direct control used about 18% of the 1200-call budget and stayed near 64% across 400 to 1200 calls, while meta-reasoning rose from 64.1% to 71.5% and used roughly 89% to 101% of the budget (range across models). With Opus 4.8, direct control doubled its calls (376 to 768) and its score rose only from 62.7% to 65.3%.
- Small budgets: Opus 4.8 meta-reasoning trails direct control at 400 calls (56.6% against 62.7%); GPT-5.5 is slightly behind at 400 too.
- Diagnostics: more worker outputs and many more dependency edges; coverage of correct candidates up in most settings (largest +20 points, Gemini on IMO); controller verdicts rank correctness better than worker confidence in several settings (Type-2 AUC 0.88 against 0.55, Gemini on IMO); frontier selection gains not uniform. Direct control's history passes one million characters on the longest Opus IMO runs, while the compact state stays at thousands to tens of thousands.

## 5. What does it not show?
- Which part helps. The comparison changes the compact state, the stages and memory access at once; there are no stage-removal, state-only or memory ablations (Appendix A).
- Efficiency. Budgets are nominal model calls; calls differ in tokens and time, and the agents use different shares of the budget. The authors draw no token, latency or equal-cost conclusions.
- Significance. Positive point estimates "do not establish significance for every difference"; several gains are under 2 points and the proof set has 30 problems. No intervals are given in the main table.
- Generality. Three frontier models, four benchmarks; no claim for weaker models, other tasks or much longer horizons.
- Why direct control plateaus. The long history may be part of it, but the authors say they "did not test it directly".
- The external comparisons are not matched: on ProgramBench the authors' two agents get a read-only git tool that Codex, Claude Code and mini-SWE Agent do not, and the external agents receive added budget accounting. The matched evidence is the direct control comparison.
- Small budgets: the overhead can make it worse (Opus 4.8 at 400 calls), and it underperforms direct control on the LongCoT-mini chess subset.

## 6. One experiment that would test the main claim
Ablate the parts against the same direct control baseline at equal budget: compact state with a single-call controller, staged control over full history, and the full design, each with confidence intervals over repeated runs, and report token cost and wall-clock time beside the call budget. That separates "the stages help" from "the short state helps" and says whether the call-budget gain survives at equal tokens.

## 8. Sharpest teaching angle for a 60 second video
Open on the matched claim: same model, same budget, only the control changed, and every main-budget score rose. Then the picture: one driver who also throws every track switch, against a signal box (the controller) that routes trains (the workers), keeps a small slate (compact state) and leaves the wagons in a shed (persistent memory). The four levers are assess, propose, evaluate, dispatch. The budget story carries the stakes: direct control stopped at about 18% of its budget with one model, and with another it doubled its calls for a small gain ("Continuing to work is not enough"). The result is the matched pair, 63.7% against 71.5% mean test-pass rate on ProgramBench with GPT-5.5, not the Codex number, and with no superlative: the largest gain in Table 1 is 9.2 points (Gemini 3.1 Pro, LongCoT-mini). Caveat on screen: some gains are small, the overhead can hurt at small budgets, and only three frontier models were tested. No cost or efficiency claim.
