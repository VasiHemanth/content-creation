# SkillSeek

Topic: [agent skill retrieval](/wiki/topics/agent-skill-retrieval.md). Source record: [arxiv-2609-38822](/raw/sources/arxiv-2609-38822.md). Short name used on screen: SkillSeek.

## 1. Identity
Guanqun Yang, Wenlong Zhang, Tian Shi, Ping Wang. arXiv 2609.38822, cs.IR. Version used: Open question (see source record).

## 2. Problem
With more than 230,000 agent skills available, choosing the right one is harder than writing one.[^abs]

## 3. Method
Two stages: a BGE-base bi-encoder retrieves candidates, a small cross-encoder reranks them. It is served over MCP.[^abs] Details of training, pool construction and prompts: Open question.

## 4. Evidence
On 89 SkillsBench tasks across a 4 x 11 grid of pool, backbone and method, SkillSeek reaches observed parity with the LLM-mediated loop of Liu et al.[^abs] BM25 alone matches or beats that loop on three of four settings. Spend per trial falls from USD 51.30 to USD 27.54.[^abs] All numbers are reported by the authors; no independent reproduction is known.

## 5. What it does not show
The authors scope the result to the SkillsBench tasks and the OpenHands harness.[^abs] Which setting BM25 loses on: Open question. Whether parity holds on other benchmarks: Open question.

## 6. One experiment
Repeat the comparison with a held-out skill pool built after the paper's pool, to see whether the first-stage recall ceiling the authors describe still explains the result.

## 7. Read next
The LLM-mediated retrieval loop of Liu et al., which this paper compares against. Citation details: Open question.

## 8. Teaching angle
"Before you let an LLM pick your tools, try plain retrieval first": cheaper by about half on this benchmark, with the same pass rate.
