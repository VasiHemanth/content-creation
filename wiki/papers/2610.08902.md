# Agent Plasticity

Topic: [agent self-improvement](/wiki/topics/agent-self-improvement.md). Authors: Harman Singh, Anton Bakhtin, Rulin Shao and 8 others (UC Berkeley, Meta Superintelligence Labs, University of Washington, Princeton). arXiv 2610.08902v1, dated 2026-10-06.

## 1. Identity
"Agent Plasticity: Measuring Self-Improvement Through Experience", arXiv 2610.08902, version v1 (the version the video was made from).[^paper]

## 2. What problem does it address?
Evaluations measure what an agent can do at one point in time, not how well it learns from experience, so two agents with the same score today can follow very different paths after the same chances to learn.[^paper]

## 3. What is the method?
A controlled protocol with frozen model weights. Each acting episode starts in a fresh context. Between checkpoints the same model reads its training games, then edits its persistent artifacts (executable tools, strategies or skills, textual memory, and files such as CLAUDE.md), and later episodes inherit them. Each checkpoint is scored on training games and on held-out games the reflection step never sees, with an easier in-distribution split and a harder out-of-distribution split. Learning cost includes model calls, rejected reflection attempts and artifact revisions, counted in dollars as a proxy for tokens. The metric, agent plasticity, is held-out score gained per unit of learning cost, reported in points per $1,000. The headline version, P_sat, fits a saturating curve to held-out ID scores against cumulative cost and divides the fitted rise by the cost of reaching 90% of it. A separate offline analysis labels each identified failure as no covering artifact, artifact available but unused, or artifact used and failure remained.[^paper]

## 4. What is the evidence?
All results are the authors' runs. Games: chess (Hard and Easy), 5 x 5 Go, 6 x 6 Hex and NetHack. Chess uses eight models; Go and Hex a subset; NetHack six lineages over checkpoints 0 to 40 through the Claude Code and Codex harnesses.[^paper]
- Hard chess, held-out ID, averaged over checkpoints 16 to 20: Claude Fable 5 from 37.5% to 73.3%, Claude Opus 5 from 25.0% to 66.4%, GPT-5.6 Sol from near 0% to 36.9%. Several models stay near their start and a few decline.
- Combined chess, Go and Hex curve (Figure 1), estimated P_sat in points per $1,000: GPT-5.6 Sol 298, Claude Opus 5 233, Claude Fable 5 57, Claude Opus 4.8 14, GPT-5.6 Luna 0 (no reliable rise). Claude Fable 5 reaches the highest fitted performance level. GPT-5.5's saturation could not be established.
- Artifact use, pooled over Hard and Easy chess: GPT-5.6 Luna takes about 98% of relevant decisions without artifact use, Gemini 3.1 Pro about 87%, Claude Opus 4.6 about 51%, GPT-5.5 about 28%, against 2 to 6% for four stronger lineages. Those four use artifacts at 94 to 98% of relevant decisions yet 83 to 99% of their remaining failures happen while a relevant artifact is in use.
- NetHack: Claude Opus 5.5 grows from about 2k to about 6k mean score, P_sat 61.75 normalized points per $1,000; the other five lineages show no reliable rise.
- Worked examples (Figure 8, p. 9): a Claude Fable 5 lineage writes its own chess engine and patches it after specific mistakes; Claude Opus 5.5 dies praying in NetHack, then adds a CLAUDE.md rule to pray only through its own controller.

## 5. What does it not show?
- A causal story. The failure and reuse labels are observational: "artifact use does not establish causal effectiveness", and the authors cannot tell a poor artifact from a poor application of a good one.
- A task-independent ranking. The authors call plasticity "a protocol-dependent measure, not a universal model ranking" and note it depends on score headroom, the learning horizon, saturation-fit assumptions and provider pricing. The 298 and 57 are point estimates quoted without an interval in the text (intervals use a parametric bootstrap, sec 2.2). Open question: how the order changes under other prices.
- The source of the gain. Fresh contexts and frozen weights isolate persistent adaptation, but they do not separate the value of environment feedback from the extra computation used to analyze experience and revise artifacts.
- Transfer across tasks. Separately trained lineages do not show that learned artifacts or improvement strategies carry from one task to another.
- Who judged the labels. The 98% and the failure categories come from Gemini 3.1 Pro reading saved games with the artifacts, checked against run logs. Open question: agreement with a human labeller; the paper does not report one.
- Scale. Evaluation sets are finite (for example 12 held-out ID games per chess checkpoint), so checkpoint-level noise is present, and each NetHack model has one lineage.

## 6. One experiment that would test the main claim
Run the same lineages with controlled artifact interventions (remove an artifact, replace it with a placebo, force its invocation) and with budget-matched reflection that receives no game feedback. The authors list both as future work; they would separate "the artifact is poor" from "the agent does not apply it" and "feedback helped" from "extra compute helped".

## 7. What should be read next?
Appendix G (the failure and reuse analyzer), the NetHack and chess examples in appendices D and E, and the related work in appendix A. On this wiki: [Dream-RSI](/wiki/papers/2609.14858.md), where an agent's exploration policy is tested by replaying past search trees, and [ContextEvo](/wiki/papers/2609.34649.md), where a harness learns what the model should see; both build self-improvement mechanisms, while this paper measures them.

## 8. Sharpest teaching angle for a 60 second video
Hook with the worked example: an agent dies while praying in NetHack, then writes itself a rule. The paper measures how fast agents learn like that, as held-out score gained per dollar. The model with the best score (Fable 5) is not the one that learns fastest per dollar (Sol, estimated 298 against 57 points per $1,000). Some agents barely use what they wrote down (about 98% of relevant decisions for one), and "writing down an instruction is not the same as following it". Caveat: the numbers depend on the setup and the prices and are not a ranking.
