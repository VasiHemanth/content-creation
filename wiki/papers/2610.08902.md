# Agent Plasticity

Topic: [agent self-improvement](/wiki/topics/agent-self-improvement.md). Authors: Harman Singh, Anton Bakhtin and 9 others (UC Berkeley, Meta Superintelligence Labs, University of Washington, Princeton). arXiv 2610.08902v1, submitted 2026-10-06.

## 1. Identity
"Agent Plasticity: Measuring Self-Improvement Through Experience", arXiv 2610.08902, version v1 (the version the video was made from).[^paper]

## 2. What problem does it address?
Agent evaluations grade what an agent can do at one moment. Agents that learn from experience need a different question answered: does performance improve and generalize, how efficiently is the improvement acquired, and where does the improvement loop break?[^paper]

## 3. What is the method?
A controlled protocol, not a new agent. Model weights stay frozen; each episode starts in a fresh context; future instances inherit self-constructed artifacts (Python tools, skills, strategies, memory) that pass executable validation. Eight frontier models play Chess, 5x5 Go, 6x6 Hex and NetHack over repeated checkpoints, scored on training, held-out ID and held-out OOD games. Plasticity is held-out score gain per dollar of learning cost; the headline Psat divides the fitted rise to saturation by the cost of reaching it. A failure analysis then classifies each decision failure by whether a relevant artifact existed, was used, and still failed.[^paper]

## 4. What is the evidence?
All results are the authors' own runs, 21 checkpoints per board-game lineage (41 for NetHack), costs from token usage.[^paper]
- Hard chess, held-out ID: Claude Fable 5 and Claude Opus 5 rise from 37.5% and 25.0% at checkpoint 0 to 73.3% and 66.4% averaged over checkpoints 16-20; GPT-5.6 Sol starts near 0% and reaches 36.9%, a similar gain that trails in absolute score.
- Combined chess+Go+Hex curve: GPT-5.6 Sol has the highest estimated Psat at 298 percentage points per $1,000 of mean per-game learning cost; Claude Opus 5 is at 233, Claude Fable 5 at 57, Claude Opus 4.8 at 14. Claude Fable 5 reaches the highest fitted endpoint: the best endpoint and the most efficient learner are different models.
- Artifact reuse: GPT-5.6 Luna takes about 98% of relevant chess decisions without artifact use, against 2-6% for the strong improvers; high-reuse models still see 83-99% of their remaining failures while an artifact is in use.

## 5. What does it not show?
- Plasticity is protocol-dependent, not a universal model ranking: estimates depend on score headroom, the learning horizon, saturation-fit assumptions, and provider pricing.[^paper]
- The failure analysis is observational; artifact use does not establish causal effectiveness, and failure after use does not separate bad artifacts from bad application.
- The setup does not isolate the contribution of environment experience from the compute spent analyzing it.
- NetHack runs start from the Claude Code and Codex harnesses, so those numbers describe the harness-agent combination, not the bare model.
- Psat is a saturation fit: GPT-5.5 had not saturated when its runs ended, so its efficiency is unestablished, not zero.

## 6. One experiment that would test the main claim
Run controlled artifact interventions (remove, replace, or force-invoke artifacts mid-lineage) to separate retrieval failures from artifact-quality failures, and rerun the board games under a second pricing schedule to test how much of the Psat ranking survives a different cost proxy.

## 7. What should be read next?
Dream-RSI ([2609.14858](/wiki/papers/2609.14858.md)) for a mechanism that this metric could grade, GEPA (reflective prompt evolution) and the continual-learning literature the paper cites (Dohare et al. 2024 on loss of plasticity).

## 8. Sharpest teaching angle for a 60 second video
Same experience, same rules, wildly different learning: the agent with the best final score (Claude Fable 5) converts experience at 57 points per $1,000 while GPT-5.6 Sol manages 298. One pair of gauges under one rain of coins carries the whole paper.
